## Context

The delivery flow was the primary and most important flow in Glovo's courier app: the sequence of screens a courier goes through on every single order. For couriers the app wasn't a nice-to-have, it was their work tool, and they used it under a very particular kind of stress, constantly on the move from one place to the next.

The goal was to make their daily routine more efficient by giving them the right information at the right time, through a succession of very clear, task-oriented steps.

## My role

I led the project. It didn't come from the roadmap: it started as a personal push, backed by my manager, to give it a go. So there was no dedicated PM, but I consulted PMs and Product Directors along the way to make sure everything stayed in line with the global product strategy.

For a period I partnered with a fellow designer from the team, which was great both to speed things up and to have someone to contrast ideas and conclusions with. Depending on the moment, I worked as a researcher, a UX designer and a UI designer.

## Process

The project ran separately from the team's daily design work: three to four months of research, followed by two to three months of design, early validation with couriers and the first final screens.

- **Mapping every type of order.** Before designing anything, I mapped the different kinds of orders couriers handle: single orders, multiple pick-ups, multiple drop-offs, supermarket orders and more. The experience had to cater for all of them, not just the common case.
- **One concept: from point A to point B.** The idea I came up with was to take the courier from point A to point B, one clear task at a time. Once that concept was in place, everything else fell into place around it.
- **Platform-agnostic design.** The flow was designed once for both platforms, so the experience was 100% consistent between iOS and Android.
- **No trade-off on the couriers' needs.** The flow had to match what couriers needed on the street, and that set the direction for every decision.

Looking back, the one thing I'd do differently is push harder for a live beta group with couriers. The engineering limitations at the time made it impossible, but testing on real deliveries earlier would have taught us even more.

## Outcome

When I left Glovo, I left the project validated, with a clear roadmap of the UI work still to design and test. Another designer took over and continued in the same direction, with great results: the new flow reduced delivery times considerably. The feedback I've had years later is that the foundations I laid out are what allowed the project to evolve and deliver successfully.

There's nothing public to link to: Glovo retired the courier app a couple of years after being acquired by Delivery Hero.

## Context

Veriff verifies people's identities for businesses. Workflows is part of Veriff's admin platform: it lets clients customise the flow their users go through during a verification session themselves. In practice it's an interactive tool for programming complex logic, such as conditions, which screens are displayed and how a session ends, without writing any code.

Before Workflows, every client who needed a custom flow required a Solutions Engineer from Veriff to build it manually. That was time-consuming and more expensive, and because each solution was bespoke, it sometimes created new problems down the road. Workflows standardises what every client can do to customise their verification sessions.

## My role

I'm the Lead Product Designer in a team of eight to ten people: front-end and back-end engineers and a product manager. I regularly present the work to product directors and the CEO, and at the monthly event in front of the entire Product & Engineering department.

## Process

Two challenges shaped the project, and the answer to the first one became the heart of the product.

- **An interface that had become obsolete.** Workflows started on top of an existing UI that no longer fitted what the product needed to become. It created friction on every decision, and that friction eventually made the case for a complete redesign.

The redesign is built around blocks. Clients compose their workflow from a set of configurable blocks that, combined, become the steps of their users' flow: API and database connectors, if/else conditionals, screens with their own configuration, and branches that split the flow. Something that used to need an engineer becomes a matter of putting the right blocks together.
- **Finding customers to talk to.** Veriff's clients are large companies, and the go-to-market team protected those relationships closely to change their customers' expectations as little as possible. Their genuine fear was that customers would read research sessions as a promise that the feature was coming soon, or feel it wasn't coming soon enough.

## Outcome

The project is still ongoing. Some parts have already been released and are testing very positively with clients.

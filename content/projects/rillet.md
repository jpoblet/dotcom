## Context

Rillet makes accounting software for accounting teams. I joined in November 2021 as employee number six and the company's first designer. The job was clear: create a product and a brand that could scale far beyond what we had.

We built everything from zero: a contract builder with smart revenue recognition and planning, journal entries booked automatically, analytics and reporting. Towards the end of my time there we started building AI into the product. While I was there, Rillet went from no clients to more than 40, and reached $0.5M in annual recurring revenue.

## My role

I designed the product, the logo and the brand, the dashboards and the design system. For most of my time I was the only designer, working with up to nine developers at once. I hired a second designer, but that role only lasted about four months, so the setup stayed the same: one designer, a lot of engineers and a product that had to keep shipping.

## Process

With one designer and nine engineers, the obvious risk was that I'd become the bottleneck, and that whatever got built without me would pile up design debt. So I changed my role. Instead of designing every screen myself, I became a design reviewer and the maintainer of the design system.

- **A robust design system first.** I invested early in a solid system of components and patterns, good enough that engineers could build screens from it with confidence.
- **Kick-off together.** For each feature, I sat down with the assigned developer, went through the ticket and sketched the solution together as rough wireframes.
- **Engineers build, without mock-ups.** The developer built the feature straight from the design system and those wireframes. I didn't design a single final screen.
- **Review it live.** Once the feature ran locally, we reviewed it together, tweaked whatever needed tweaking, designed and documented any new components the system needed, and pushed it to production.

That let us ship complex features quickly, without me holding anything up and without creating design debt. Looking back, it was an "agentic" way of working before the AI era: I set up the system and the rules, and others produced the work within them.

## Outcome

The framework, the logo and brand, the dashboard screens and the design system I put in place stayed almost unchanged for years after I left. Rillet only rebranded and started moving to a new visual direction in October 2026.

The company has done very well since. After I left, it raised a $70M Series B in August 2025 and a $100M Series C at a $1B valuation in August 2026, for over $200M raised in total, with investors including Sequoia, Andreessen Horowitz and ICONIQ.

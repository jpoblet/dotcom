---
title: Travels
---

Travel has always been a constant thread in my life — a way to explore cultures, challenge comfort zones, and live stories worth telling.

Among many adventures, the most transformative was a five-month overland journey across Africa. Starting in northern Ethiopia in October 2019, I traveled all the way to Cape Town, South Africa in early 2020 — entirely by land. No flights, only local transport, and border crossings on foot. The route took me through Kenya, Tanzania (including Zanzibar), Mozambique, Zimbabwe, Zambia, Botswana, Namibia, and finally into South Africa.

This wasn't just a personal adventure — it also shaped my professional journey. Along the way, I continued working remotely as a designer, collaborating with local businesses and initiatives. In Ewaso Ngiro, a small Maasai community near the edge of the Masai Mara, I ran prioritisation workshops and created websites for two local organisations. The image of peeling post-it notes with the Maasai in the shadow of the savannah is something I'll never forget. Later in Namibia, while staying at Okasewa Ranch near Witvlei, I lived a dual rhythm: moving horses and herding cattle by day, and designing the ranch's website by night — all while keeping an eye out for baboons.

Beyond Africa, I've been fortunate to explore a wide range of cultures and terrains: Lanzarote in the Canary Islands, Vietnam, Cambodia, South Korea, Bali, New Zealand, Eastern Australia, Tasmania, Mexico, India, and the Maldives.

More recently, together with my wife Chiara and our Weimaraner, Ringo, we've been traveling in our camper van. Since April 2026 our son Teo comes along too: we all travel together. It's become our little weekend escape — taking us across Spain, Italy, France, Portugal, Slovenia, Croatia, and all the way to Sardinia and Sicily. It's how we recharge and reconnect with nature, people, and each other.

The list of future destinations continues to grow. At the top: Iceland, Patagonia, the Altiplano of Bolivia and Peru — and hopefully, another chapter in Africa, this time exploring its western regions.

## Weekend MVPs

I like building small, random products to keep up with new technologies. I could learn the same things on courses or bootcamps, but I'd rather do it with a specific problem to solve, and by launching the result into the wild.

That habit turned into a list I call "Weekend MVPs": ideas that shouldn't take more than 10 to 15 hours to build into a working, launched MVP, a weekend at most. I pick them for their simplicity, technical viability, low effort and potentially high impact. I rarely build something for the money. It's more for fun, and for the chance that one of them becomes a viral hit.

Some of them stay in a GitHub repo forever, because along the way I discover the idea isn't that stimulating after all. One example: during the COVID lockdowns I built a world map made of circles, each one sized by the number of COVID cases in that place. This was before AI coding agents, and I lacked a lot of the technical knowledge to take it to a product mature enough to launch. I parked it, COVID passed, and it no longer made sense to pursue. But I learnt how to pull data from an API into a map library, on a front end that was responsive and interactive.

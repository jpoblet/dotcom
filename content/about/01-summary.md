I'm a product designer who builds. I've been designing digital products for {{years_since:2016-11}} years, and designing in general for {{years_since:2011}}: furniture and brand identities came first, then apps and platforms. My day job is Lead Product Designer at Veriff, and in my own time I take products from idea to launch on my own: an iOS game about flags (What The Flag), a Chrome extension for sports fans (Legend Numbers) and a whole platform for athletes and race organisers (AthleteID). They're all at the launch stage right now, so there's no usage data to share yet.

Honestly, I don't know where the designer ends and the engineer starts any more. Since AI entered its agentic era, that line has become really blurry. I've always been curious about how things work, and that curiosity has made me a better designer: knowing how something will be built gives me a list of constraints straight away, and I see those constraints as guides towards a design that works.

Now that I can build a product from start to finish by myself, I feel less like a designer or an engineer and more like a product builder. It forces me to think more strategically and less about execution. To me, the question isn't "how well can you design or build this?" any more. It's "how good is the idea, and how well thought through is it?". Time to market is so short now that if I had to put a title on what I do, I'd say it's closer to "inventor" than "designer".

## About titles

Titles get confusing with a profile like mine, so here's how I think about them:

- **Designer, Founder & Product Builder** is the broad description of what I do, in my day job and on my own.
- **Staff Product Designer** is the level and role I fit in a company: someone who shapes product strategy and what gets built, not only how it looks, and who brings a founder's attitude to it.
- **Design Engineer** (or Frontend Designer) is how I describe the bridge I build between product design and front-end development. I design in code as much as in Figma.
- **Inventor** is the side I care about most: coming up with the idea in the first place, and making sure it's well thought through.

title: Leadership and mentoring
---

Leadership is something I really enjoy, especially mentoring people who are just starting out and taking them under my wing. It connects with something I've done for years outside work: coaching roller hockey players in Melbourne. Helping someone get better, on a rink or in a design file, works in much the same way.

## Glovo: a mentorship programme for juniors

At Glovo I led a mentorship programme for junior designers starting out in their careers. We hired two people on six-month contracts, and both of them ended up with full-time contracts. A few of the rituals I designed for it:

- **Regular catch-ups**, so nothing waited weeks to be talked about.
- **Completely honest, open feedback**, in both directions.
- **Listening to what the mentees wanted to learn**, and adapting their projects to it, rather than handing them a fixed plan.

## Rillet: hiring and working with a senior designer

At Rillet I hired a second designer for the team and led their onboarding and project assignments. They were more senior than the juniors I'd mentored before, with their own established processes and experience, so I learnt to adapt to a colleague's strengths instead of teaching my own way of working. The role lasted about four months.

## Leading without managing

Most of my leadership has happened without a team reporting to me: setting the design direction on a project, raising the bar through design systems and reviews, and bringing engineers and product managers along with an idea. At Rillet, the way I worked with developers was itself a form of leadership: I set up a system so that nine engineers could ship well-designed features without waiting for me.

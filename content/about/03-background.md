My background is rooted in Industrial Design. I began my academic journey with a BA in Arts at ELISAVA, School of Design in Barcelona, followed by a second degree in Design. This dual foundation allowed me to merge industrial knowledge with creative processes, laying the groundwork for a multidisciplinary approach to design. From 2006 to 2011, I immersed myself in the world of design academia.

But on 24 September 2011, with a backpack and a sense of curiosity, I moved to Melbourne — initially just for a few months. That temporary move turned into {{duration:2011-09/2019-04}} that transformed me. In Melbourne, I encountered experience design in a way that was completely new to me — especially in the digital space. I shifted from designing physical objects, such as furniture at Tubeworks Furniture, and creating visual identities for local brands like North East Coffee and Naked Espresso, to solving digital user experience problems. What was then known simply as "UX design" became my new focus.

My curiosity led me to enroll in two 6-week courses at Tractor Design School. Before even completing them, I was offered my first opportunity in the field — a one-week contract at Tigerspike. Initially hesitant about the risk, a conversation with Tristan Hewat, then Head of Design at Tigerspike Melbourne, convinced me to leave my job as a Production Coordinator at a local sportswear manufacturer. That one-week contract turned into two, then three, and eventually a full-time role. I had cracked into the world of product design.

As the most junior designer in the office, I approached the role with humility — listening, observing, and learning from everyone around me. My visual design skills sharpened immensely during this time.

After leaving Tigerspike in 2018, I joined Isobar in Melbourne as a UI designer on a month-to-month contract, which continued until April 2019. At Isobar, I worked on projects for clients like Bridgestone, Jetstar and the Australian Gas Network. The high-paced agency environment taught me the value of working smart, managing expectations, and handling stakeholders.

Between Tigerspike and Isobar, I took a break and followed an unexpected path: working at a summer camp in Hawai'i. Kayaking in Kealakekua Bay among wild dolphins, swimming alongside honu (Hawaiian for turtle) at Kua Bay, hiking Mauna Kea, and surfing the waves at Kona Beach — those few months were nothing short of magic.

## After Melbourne

In 2019 I left Melbourne for a five-month overland trip across Africa, from Ethiopia to Cape Town (more about it in my travels). When I came back, I joined Glovo in April 2020 as a Product Designer on the courier app, where I also led the research and redesign of its delivery flow.

From Glovo I went to Rillet in November 2021, as employee number six and their first designer. The job was to create a product and a brand that could scale, so I set up the foundations of the design system and the design patterns.

In October 2024 I joined Veriff as Lead Product Designer, in charge of the Expansion area. Among many things, that means thinking ahead of the trends, and coming up with and testing products and solutions the company could be building in the future. Workflows is one of the projects I've led there.

Alongside all of that, AI has changed how I work. I now design and build my own products from start to finish, which is why I describe myself as a Design Engineer, or more and more, a product builder.

My background is in industrial design. I studied at ELISAVA, the School of Design and Engineering in Barcelona: first a BA in Engineering in Industrial Design (2006 to 2009), then a degree in Product Design (2009 to 2011). Having both gave me the engineering side and the creative side, and I've mixed the two ever since.

## Melbourne

On 24 September 2011 I moved to Melbourne with a backpack, planning to stay a few months. I stayed {{duration:2011-09/2019-04}}, and it changed me. I started out designing physical things: furniture at Tubeworks Furniture, and visual identities for local brands like North East Coffee and Naked Espresso. Then I came across experience design, especially in digital products, and it was completely new to me. What everyone then simply called "UX design" became my new focus.

I signed up for two six-week courses at Tractor Design School, and before I'd even finished them I was offered my first job in the field: a one-week contract at Tigerspike (now Concentrix Catalyst). I was working as a Production Coordinator at a local sportswear manufacturer at the time, and I wasn't sure about taking the risk. A conversation with Tristan Hewat, then Head of Design at Tigerspike Melbourne, convinced me. One week turned into two, then three, and then a full-time role. I was in.

I was the most junior designer in the office, so I listened, watched and learnt from everyone around me. My visual design got much sharper in those years.

After Tigerspike (now Concentrix Catalyst) I took a break and did something unexpected: I worked at a summer camp in Hawai'i. Kayaking with wild dolphins in Kealakekua Bay, swimming next to honu (Hawaiian for turtle) at Kua Bay, hiking Mauna Kea and surfing in Kona. Those few months were pure magic.

Back in Melbourne, I joined Isobar as a UI designer on a month-to-month contract, from November 2018 to April 2019, working for clients like Bridgestone, Jetstar and the Australian Gas Network. The agency pace taught me to work smart, manage expectations and handle stakeholders.

## A World Championship and Africa

In April 2019 I left Isobar and Melbourne and went back to Barcelona to train full-time for the Roller Hockey World Championships. I trained for almost four months with a local club, and at the end of July I played the competition representing Australia. After that I took almost three months to rest and get ready for Africa, doing some freelance work for Australian clients remotely from Barcelona.

In October 2019 I set off on a five-month overland trip across Africa, from Ethiopia to Cape Town (more about it in my travels). At the last stop, in South Africa, I applied for a job at Glovo and even did my first interview from there. I flew from Cape Town on 11 March 2020, landed in Barcelona on the 12th, and the country went into Covid lockdown on the 13th. The hiring process went on for several more weeks, all from home.

## Glovo, Rillet and Veriff

I started at Glovo on 22 April 2020 as a Product Designer on the courier app, where I also led the research and redesign of its delivery flow, and a mentorship programme for junior designers.

In November 2021 I joined Rillet as employee number six and their founding designer. Rillet makes accounting software for accounting teams, and I designed the product and the brand from zero, along with the design system that let a single designer keep up with nine engineers. We parted ways on 27 August 2024: the go-to-market side wasn't getting enough traction, major feature work was paused, and there wasn't enough design work left to justify my role. After a good run, I also wanted a different kind of project in a different sector.

I took the next couple of months to find a company and a role that matched where I wanted my career to go: leading the design of a new generation of products in a tech company. Veriff's culture, and the real interest its design manager showed in my profile, made the decision easy. I started on 21 October 2024 as Lead Product Designer, in charge of the Expansion area. Expansion was set up as the vertical that would research, test and develop the products and solutions Veriff didn't have yet: areas like KYB (verifying businesses, not just people), support for unstructured documents, and new emerging technologies. When the vertical lost traction, I moved to the Workflows project, which I've led since.

Since 22 November 2025 I've been on medical leave (more on that in the section about cancer and becoming a dad). I'm following my doctors' advice and will go back to my role as soon as they see me fit.

Alongside all of that, AI has changed how I work. I now design and build my own products from start to finish, which is why I call myself a product builder.

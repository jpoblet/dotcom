---
title: How I think
---

I'm a curious, intuitive thinker, and I like challenging the way things are usually done. Rules are a starting point for me, not a limit. Thinking outside the box isn't a technique I switch on, it's just my default mode.

The funny thing is that I love constraints. The more freedom a design has, the more chances it has of missing its goal. You need constraints, whether they come from the technology, from what users prefer or from the brand, and I treat them as guides rather than obstacles. A few examples from my own work:

- **Glovo:** before designing anything for the courier app's delivery flow, I mapped every type of order couriers handle: single orders, multiple pick-ups, multiple drop-offs, supermarket orders. Those cases became the constraints, and the concept that came out of them was simple: take the courier from point A to point B, one clear task at a time.
- **Veriff:** Workflows lets clients program complex verification logic without writing code. Rather than giving them a blank canvas, I gave them a set of configurable blocks (connectors, conditionals, screens, branches) that snap together. Fewer possibilities, much more power.
- **Legend Numbers:** the extension had one strict rule: never change anything on the page it runs on. That constraint shaped everything, from how it decides whether a number is a shirt number or a date to how it underlines it.
- **What The Flag:** I decided from day one that the game wouldn't collect any personal data. No accounts, everything stays on the phone. It made some features harder, and the product better.
- **Rillet:** as the founding designer, I knew the product would grow far beyond what we had, so the first thing I invested in was a solid design system and clear patterns that would outlast me.

People who've worked with me usually mention my intuition, how clearly I think, and how I can take a messy, complex problem and turn it into something simple. I bring both the imagination and the structure.

---
title: What I'm looking for next
---

## Moving into management

Having reached the highest IC level, I really want to move into the management track now. Every time I've had the chance to manage and mentor other designers, I've not only enjoyed it, the projects and the work did really well too. At Glovo I designed and led the internship programme that took two interns on six-month contracts and turned them into full-time designers on the team. At Rillet I hired and managed another designer, which meant splitting the work, guiding them and still guaranteeing the quality of what we shipped. It's something I've been eager to jump into, and it's what I want to pursue next.

## The kind of company

I'm finding that the larger the company, the harder it is to make change happen. Smaller teams are easier to steer, and your points and proposals reach the C-level much faster. I really enjoyed that close relationship with the founders at Rillet.

The domain matters less to me than the culture and the objectives. What I'd say no to is gambling, or any other space whose products can be addictive or harmful to the people using them. As a designer I want to change little bits of the world for the better, not the other way round.

## Remote, hybrid or office

I've worked fully remotely at Rillet and Veriff, and at Glovo too because of COVID. What I care about is that going to an office actually means something. I've found myself in an office where all my interactions were still remote, because the people I worked with weren't there. If I'm going to work remotely from an office, I'd rather stay at home, where I can organise my personal life better and save the commute. With my son, being close to the kindergarten makes things easier too.

But if going to the office helps me connect better with the work and get to a faster, better result, I'd fully commit to it.

## Why join a company instead of founding one

I'd love to found and run my own company eventually. The realistic part is that building and launching a product is hard, and it often needs a bit of luck. If one of my own projects gets enough traction to work on it full-time, I'll definitely consider it.

For now I'm having fun with what I do, and I'm still learning something new every day. My personal projects are mostly a way to test new tools, learn new technologies and have real case studies that show what I can do.
